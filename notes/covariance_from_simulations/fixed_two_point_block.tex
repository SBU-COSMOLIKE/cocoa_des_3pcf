\section{Keeping the analytical two-point covariance fixed}
\label{sec:fixed-two-point}
\paragraph{The scientific assumption in this experiment.}
We now assume that the analytical two-point covariance is exact. It includes
the Gaussian, connected non-Gaussian and super-sample contributions. We do
not fit this block to simulations or vary it to assess model uncertainty.
The simulations are used to learn the cross-covariance with three-point
measurements and the covariance of the three-point measurements themselves.
This is a different use of simulation information from correcting every
entry of a joint matrix.

\paragraph{First specify what a row measures.}
Let the two-point measurement vector be $x$ and the three-point vector be $y$.
For example, the first entry of $x$ could be the mean tangential-shear
correlation in one angular interval for one source-bin pair. Its definition
includes the angular edges, the pair weights and the source bins. The joint
measurement is
\begin{equation}
 d=\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Subtract the ensemble mean before defining its covariance. The resulting
block matrix is
\begin{equation}
 C=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B^{\mathsf T}&D\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Here $A$ describes fluctuations in $x$ alone. The rectangular matrix $B$
describes how a fluctuation in $x$ accompanies a fluctuation in $y$. Finally,
$D$ describes fluctuations in $y$ alone. We keep $A$ fixed. An element of
$B$ is not a dimensionless correlation coefficient: its units are the
product of the units of the corresponding two measurements.

\paragraph{Why the existing map journal cannot simply receive a new block.}
The current map-generation journal stores eight raw squared aperture
moments followed by eight raw cubed aperture moments. A squared aperture
moment is obtained by filtering a map around each chosen center, squaring
the filtered value, and averaging over those centers. A binned $\xi_\pm$
measurement instead averages shear products over pairs in a separation bin.
Both are two-point statistics, but they are different random variables.
The DES Y3 production covariance runner computes the latter. Its rows cannot
replace the former just because both quantities are called two-point
statistics. Matching matrix dimensions would not cure this difference.

\paragraph{A two-number example of changing the measurement.}
Suppose two measured correlations are collected in $x$. Define a new
measurement by a fixed weighted sum:
\begin{equation}
 q=w_1x_1+w_2x_2.
\end{equation}
Subtract its mean. Each centered correlation retains the same weight:
\begin{equation}
 q-\E[q]=w_1(x_1-\E[x_1])+w_2(x_2-\E[x_2]).
\end{equation}
Squaring and taking the expectation produces three terms:
\begin{equation}
 \Var(q)=w_1^2A_{11}+2w_1w_2A_{12}+w_2^2A_{22}.
\end{equation}
Thus even this simple change of observable changes its covariance. For a
matrix of fixed weights the corresponding rule is
\begin{equation}
 q=Wx.
\end{equation}
Each covariance axis receives its own copy of the weights:
\begin{equation}
 A_q=WAW^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
The rule is exact only when $W$ actually implements the measurement. A
finite-center, masked map aperture estimate cannot be identified with a
weighted sum of coarse angular bins without establishing that relation.
Our route is therefore to measure the required $\xi_\pm$ rows from the saved
maps and pair them with three-point measurements from the same maps. Saving
the maps allows this new measurement without generating new skies.

\paragraph{What the production run supplies.}
The ordinary DES Y3 covariance runner saves the Gaussian, super-sample and
connected non-Gaussian matrices separately. Their sum supplies the fixed
analytical block after selecting the desired shear rows in the declared
order. Galaxy-density and galaxy--shear rows of its full output are not
part of this cosmic-shear block. The simulation measurement must use the
same angular intervals and source-bin pairs. Survey area, source curves,
noise convention and cosmology must also describe the intended experiment;
this is input matching, not a test of the assumed accuracy of $A$.
Until these measurements exist, the first-1000 aperture results remain a
separate demonstration and are not results for a fixed production block.

\paragraph{Remove the part of the three-point fluctuation predicted by $x$.}
In the following derivation both vectors are centered. Define the residual
\begin{equation}
 r=y-B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}x.
\end{equation}
Multiplying this expression by its transpose gives four terms. Writing all
four prevents losing a cross term:
\begin{equation}
 rr^{\mathsf T}=yy^{\mathsf T}
 -yx^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}B
 -B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}xy^{\mathsf T}
 +B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}xx^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}B.
\end{equation}
Take expectations using the definitions of $A$, $B$ and $D$:
\begin{equation}
 \E[rr^{\mathsf T}]=D-B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}B
 -B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}B+B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}AA^{-1}B.
\end{equation}
The last term cancels one of the two subtractions. The residual covariance,
also called the Schur complement, is therefore
\begin{equation}
 S=D-B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}B.
\end{equation}
This identity concerns linear prediction. Calling it the full conditional
covariance requires additional distributional assumptions, such as joint
Gaussianity. The block-inverse algebra does not need those assumptions.

\paragraph{Why estimating the cross term twice produces a bias.}
Write an unbiased estimate of $B$ as its true value plus a random error:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat B=B+E,\qquad \E[E]=0.
\end{equation}
Insert this into the quadratic term and expand before taking expectations:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}\widehat B
 =B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}B+B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}E
 +E^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}B+E^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}E.
\end{equation}
The two terms linear in the error average to zero. The last does not:
\begin{equation}
 \E[\widehat B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}\widehat B]
 =B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}B+\E[E^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}E].
\end{equation}
For any vector $v$, the extra contribution obeys
\begin{equation}
 v^{\mathsf T}\E[E^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}E]v
 =\E[(Ev)^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}(Ev)]\geq0.
\end{equation}
Consequently, simply subtracting the noisy square biases the residual
covariance downward. Treating $A$ as exact does not remove this bias.

\paragraph{An explicit unbiased cross-product from independent pairs.}
Pair independent simulation vectors without reusing a vector. For pair $i$
define the two differences
\begin{equation}
 u_i=(x_{2i}-x_{2i-1})/\sqrt{2},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 v_i=(y_{2i}-y_{2i-1})/\sqrt{2}.
\end{equation}
The difference cancels the unknown mean. Independence of the two maps makes
its covariance equal to that of one map. The cross-product
\begin{equation}
 b_i=u_iv_i^{\mathsf T}
\end{equation}
has expectation $B$. Distinct pairs are independent, so for $i\ne j$,
\begin{equation}
 \E[b_i^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}b_j]=B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}B.
\end{equation}
With $m$ pairs, average over ordered distinct pairs:
\begin{equation}
 Q_B=\frac{1}{m(m-1)}\sum_{i\ne j}b_i^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}b_j.
\end{equation}
This expression is symmetric because every ordered pair appears together
with its transpose. It can be evaluated without a double loop by first
including all pairs and then subtracting the equal-index terms:
\begin{equation}
 Q_B=\frac{(\sum_i b_i)^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}(\sum_i b_i)
 -\sum_i b_i^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}b_i}{m(m-1)}.
\end{equation}
The ordinary sample covariance of the $y$ vectors is unbiased for $D$.
Subtracting $Q_B$ therefore gives an unbiased estimate of $S$. This does not
make its inverse unbiased, nor guarantee positivity in one finite sample.
Higher powers must again use distinct random factors; squaring this estimate
would reintroduce repeated-factor noise.

\paragraph{Keep the uncertainty statement attached to the right object.}
The known $A$ removes sampling noise from that covariance block. It does not
make the upper-left precision block equal to $A^{-1}$: the joint analysis
also uses information carried by the correlations with $y$. Block inversion
gives
\begin{equation}
 C^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}
 A^{-1}+A^{-1}BS^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}&-A^{-1}BS^{-1}\\
 -S^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}&S^{-1}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Simulation corrections to $B$ and $D$ can therefore change every block of
the precision while the two-point covariance remains exactly fixed.
An unbiased finite precision polynomial, its inverse, and a covariance
constrained to retain $A$ are different estimators. Their properties must
be checked separately rather than inherited from one another by naming.

\paragraph{Extend the distinct-factor construction to orders two, four and six.}
For each independent pair, assemble a joint matrix using the exact known
block and the measured cross and three-point products:
\begin{equation}
 Z_i=\begin{pmatrix}A&u_iv_i^{\mathsf T}\\v_iu_i^{\mathsf T}&v_iv_i^{\mathsf T}\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Its expectation is the desired joint covariance. An individual $Z_i$ need
not be positive definite; it is an unbiased matrix contribution, not a
covariance to invert. Choose an independently fixed positive reference
$M=LL^{\mathsf T}$ with the same leading block $A$. Define
\begin{equation}
 T_i=I-L^{-1}Z_iL^{-\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
Its expectation is a deterministic residual matrix:
\begin{equation}
 T=I-L^{-1}CL^{-\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
For different indices, independence lets expectations factor in the written
order, even though the individual matrices do not commute:
\begin{equation}
 \E[T_{i_1}T_{i_2}\cdots T_{i_k}]=T^k.
\end{equation}
We average products with increasing indices, then symmetrize. This is a
specified subset of ordered products, not an assertion that their realized
values are equal. Each product has the same expectation. Starting with
$U_0=I$, the update after adding pair $m$ is
\begin{equation}
 U_k^{(m)}=\frac{m-k}{m}U_k^{(m-1)}
 +\frac{k}{m}U_{k-1}^{(m-1)}T_m.
\end{equation}
The first fraction counts subsets not using the newest pair. The second
counts subsets using it. Update degrees in descending order so the same
pair cannot enter twice. The order-$r$ precision estimate is
\begin{equation}
 P_r=L^{-\mathsf T}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{r}
 \frac{U_k+U_k^{\mathsf T}}{2}\right]L^{-1},\qquad r=2,4,6.
\end{equation}
This estimates the corresponding finite polynomial without repeated-factor
bias. It is not unbiased for the infinite inverse. The deterministic series
requires all eigenvalues of $T$ to have magnitude below one to converge.
We do not repair failed or indefinite estimates by clipping eigenvalues.

\paragraph{Retain the covariance block exactly after truncation.}
Inverting a finite joint precision polynomial does not in general return
exactly $A$ in its leading covariance block. To impose that requirement,
we use a conditional parameterization and report its extra nonlinearity.
Partition the raw precision and define
\begin{equation}
 K=(P_r)_{yy}.
\end{equation}
This block estimates the precision of the residual three-point fluctuation.
The cross block then supplies the linear prediction coefficients:
\begin{equation}
 H=-K^{-1}(P_r)_{yx}.
\end{equation}
Only if $K$ is positive definite do we form the constrained covariance:
\begin{equation}
 C_r^{\rm fixed}=\begin{pmatrix}A&AH^{\mathsf T}\\HA&K^{-1}+HAH^{\mathsf T}\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Its leading block is exactly the supplied matrix. Its cross block is
$AH^{\mathsf T}$, and its residual covariance is $K^{-1}$: subtracting the
cross product cancels the last term of the lower block. Hence positivity
follows from positive $A$ and $K$. This transformation is nonlinear. The
unbiasedness proof applies to the raw polynomial, not to this constrained
covariance or its precision. Independent validation tests this additional
estimation effect along with truncation and sampling error. At the exact
population inverse, the transformation returns the original covariance.

\paragraph{The first production calculation and the existing-map test.}
The DES Y3 production calculation completed in 109.97 seconds on four
threads, excluding its 1.20-second initialization. It saved separate
$900\times900$ Gaussian, SSC and cNG matrices and their total. For a bounded
joint test we selected source-bin-four $\xi_+$ in angular bins 12--15 of
20 logarithmic bins spanning 2.5--250 arcmin. Those four rows are paired
with the eight cubic aperture moments from the same primary map. This
12-entry vector is an explicitly defined preliminary statistic, not the
published joint 480-entry vector.

\paragraph{What can already be measured without processing new maps.}
There are 32 Rafael primary-map labels with the four pair measurements
already recorded. Joining their labels to the saved cubic moments gives
32 joint vectors. The exact analytical $A$ is extracted from the production
total without rescaling. The cubic reference is twice the archived
Gaussian aperture reference; it is not fitted to these 32 joint vectors.
Second order gives a positive constrained covariance. Its largest cross-block
difference from the direct sample, divided by the corresponding direct
standard deviations, is 0.656. The largest similarly normalized difference
in the cubic block is 5.712. These are large discrepancies, not evidence of
an improvement. Fourth and sixth orders give non-positive residual precision
blocks, so no constrained covariance is reported for them. The second-order
analytical block changes by exactly zero.

\paragraph{How to interpret that result.}
Thirty-two maps provide only sixteen independent-pair factors, conditional
on the primary labels identifying independent skies. The high orders can
be noisy, and the starting cubic reference may be far from the required
joint covariance. These failures do not question the assumed exact $A$;
they show that a finite-order correction of the remaining blocks need not
be usable for this ensemble and reference. The archived survey calculation
matches the angular-row definitions; equality of all simulation and survey
inputs has not been established. We therefore do not label this result a
validated DES likelihood covariance.

\paragraph{The larger test and what is still pending.}
We are measuring the same four $\xi_+$ bins on the saved GPTG maps. The
first 1,000 joint vectors set the corrections and direct sample covariance;
the next 500 give an independent quadratic-minus-log-determinant score.
Neither the reference nor the order is tuned on those 500 maps. The score
does not invert their sample covariance or assume that the direct inverse
is true. These new measurements, unlike the existing aperture journal,
supply the observable needed for the analytical block. They do not require
regenerating any sky. The comparison remains conditional on the fitted
GPTG distribution and the declared survey inputs.

\paragraph{Checks of the new algebra.}
Explicit enumeration of every increasing-index product of six factors
agrees with the recurrence through sixth order. The cross-square subtraction
agrees with the direct sum over unequal indices. In 512 Gaussian experiments
with 128 vectors and an exactly known fixed block, the largest entrywise
mean discrepancy from each deterministic finite-polynomial target is 2.60
Monte Carlo standard errors or less. Every returned constrained covariance
retains the supplied block exactly and satisfies the matrix inverse identity.
These checks establish the implementation's algebra; they do not turn the
failed 32-map high-order estimates into successful ones.
